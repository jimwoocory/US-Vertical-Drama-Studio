# Hook, Payoff, Cliffhanger Library

## Functional taxonomy

Choose functions that serve the current promise and transaction; do not copy surface events.

| Function | Hook | Payoff | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Danger | imminent harm or impossible exposure | survival with a new cost | threat arrives with altered leverage |
| Deadline | clock, expiring access, or closing window | costly progress before time runs out | new deadline or proof the clock was misread |
| Power | visible status contradiction or challenge | leverage is won, spent, or reversed | a stronger counterclaim changes who can act |
| Information | contradiction, proof, or suspicion | answer changes the plan | answer exposes a larger or more personal question |
| Relationship | loyalty test, betrayal signal, or unwanted alliance | choice clarifies a bond at a price | a relationship decision becomes unavoidable |
| Moral choice | no-clean-win dilemma | character chooses and pays | consequence forces the next moral choice |
| World rule | limit, ritual, or institution blocks the goal | rule is used, bent, or understood | rule reveals a hidden cost or exception |

## Earned retention anchors

A hook is earned when it grows from the prior state and quickly gives the viewer a legible destabilizing question. A payoff is earned when it answers, transforms, or cashes a planted question through character choice and a changed state. A cliffhanger is earned when new information, danger, deadline, or irreversible action alters the next available choice.

**fake withholding** is not a cliffhanger: hiding basic context the scene should supply, delaying an answer without changing state, cutting away from an unresolved action only to resume unchanged, or labeling a setup a payoff. Repair it with cause, consequence, and a specific next-episode question.

## Anti-copy rules

- Do not repeat a hook/payoff/cliffhanger signature inside its cooldown window without a changed mechanism plus two novelty dimensions from the repetition ledger.
- Do not use the same overheard threat, public humiliation, mystery caller, rescue-at-the-last-second, or secret-document shape as a substitute for escalation.
- Preserve the series promise, but rotate the dramatic transaction: a power hook can become an information payoff, a relationship payoff can create a moral cliffhanger, and so on.
- Record the anchor's question, answer/state change, and next choice in the episode beat sheet so the Doctor can assess whether it is earned.
